## 📂 Dataset

The dataset contains information about 100 students, including:

- Student ID
- Gender
- Age
- Attendance
- Math Score
- Science Score
- English Score

---

## 🔧 Analysis Performed

### 1. Total Score

Calculated the total marks obtained by each student:

**Total Score = Math + Science + English**

### 2. Average Score

Calculated the average academic score:

**Average Score = Total Score / 3**

### 3. Pass / Fail Classification

Students were classified based on their average score.

- Average Score ≥ 40 → Pass
- Average Score < 40 → Fail

### 4. Grade Classification

Students were assigned grades based on their performance using Nested IF logic.

### 5. Student Ranking

Students were ranked according to their average score using Excel ranking functions.

### 6. Attendance Classification

Attendance was categorized into different levels to identify students with:

- Excellent attendance
- Good attendance
- Low attendance

### 7. Conditional Formatting

Conditional formatting was used to identify:

- High-performing students
- Students with low attendance
- Students close to failing
- Students who failed

---

## 📊 Key Questions Answered

The analysis focused on questions such as:

1. Who is the highest-performing student?
2. What is the average class score?
3. How many students failed?
4. How many students have attendance below 75%?
5. Which subject has the lowest average score?
6. Do students with different attendance levels show differences in average performance?

---

## 💡 Key Insights

- The highest-performing student achieved a total score of **296/300**.
- The average class score was approximately **70.49%**.
- **1 student** failed based on the defined pass/fail criteria.
- **21 students** had attendance below 75%.
- **Science** had the lowest average score among the three subjects.
- Students with excellent attendance had the highest average score in this dataset.
- The analysis shows patterns between attendance and academic performance, but it does not establish causation.

## 🧠 Key Learning

The biggest lesson from Day 3 was:

> **Data analysis is not just about calculating numbers. It is about interpreting what those numbers mean.**

I learned to move through the following process:

**Question → Calculate → Verify → Interpret → Insight**

I also learned that a correlation or pattern in a dataset does not automatically mean that one factor causes another.
