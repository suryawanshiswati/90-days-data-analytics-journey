## 🎯 Objective

---

## 📚 What I Learned

### 1. What is Data Analytics?

Data Analytics involves examining, cleaning, transforming and interpreting data to discover useful information, draw conclusions and support decision-making.

### 2. What Does a Data Analyst Do?

A Data Analyst collects, cleans and interprets data to help businesses make better decisions.

I also learned that analysts can work on different types of business problems, including:

* Sales and marketing problems
* Business and operational problems
* Product and technology problems

---

## 🔄 Data Analytics Workflow

The workflow I learned is:

**1. Define the Business Problem**
↓
**2. Understand the Data**
↓
**3. Clean the Data**
↓
**4. Analyze the Data**
↓
**5. Visualize the Results**
↓
**6. Find Insights**
↓
**7. Communicate Recommendations**

This helped me understand that data analysis is not simply about performing calculations. The business problem should guide the analysis.

---

## 🧹 Data Cleaning

I learned that data cleaning involves identifying and fixing issues in data so that it becomes accurate, consistent and complete.

### Why is data cleaning important?

* Ensures accuracy
* Improves efficiency
* Builds trust in the data

I also learned the difference between:

**Data Cleaning:** Fixing errors and preparing the dataset.

**Data Analysis:** Finding patterns and answering questions using trustworthy data.

---

## 💡 Finding vs Insight

One of the important concepts I learned today was the difference between a finding and an insight.

### Finding

A finding describes **what happened** in the data.

### Insight

An insight goes deeper by considering **why it happened and what could be done next**.

This helped me understand that the goal of analytics is not only to report numbers but to provide information that can support decisions.

# 🧪 Practical Challenge — HR Analytics

For the practical challenge, I worked with an **HR_ANALYTICS** dataset.

### Dataset Purpose

The dataset contains employee-related information such as:

* Employee ID
* Department
* Job Role
* Monthly Income
* Performance Rating
* Overtime
* Years at Company

The purpose was to analyze employee details based on different parameters.

### Potential Data-Quality Problems Identified

I identified five possible issues:

1. Duplicate Employee IDs
2. Incorrect data formatting
3. Blank or missing cells
4. Spelling mistakes
5. Incorrect date formatting

I also connected these problems with relevant Excel approaches such as Remove Duplicates, Format Cells, Go To Special and spelling checks.

---

## 📊 Business Questions

I created five questions that could be explored using the HR dataset:

1. What is the total number of employees?
2. Which are the top 10 employees having the highest salaries?
3. Which Sales Representatives have a performance rating greater than 3 and overtime equal to Yes?
4. Which employees have a salary greater than 5000?
5. Which employees have TrainingTimesLastYear less than 2 and Performance Rating less than 3?

---

I expect the **Sales Representative** job role to have the highest average salary among the departments.

### Hypothesis 2

I expect the **26–36 age group** to have a higher YearsAtCompany value than the 18–25 age group.

These hypotheses can later be tested using data analysis.

---

## 🧠 Key Takeaway

My biggest takeaway from Day 6 is that a Data Analyst should not immediately start calculating numbers.

The process should begin with:

> **Business Problem → Data → Cleaning → Analysis → Insights → Decision**

I am learning to focus not only on **what the data says**, but also on **what business question the data can help answer**.
